## Part A: Trigonometric proofs

Background fact [V, Shene]: Loomis (The Pythagorean Proposition) claimed "there are no trigonometric proofs... Trigonometry is because the Pythagorean Theorem is." This is the circularity objection. Every item below is judged by whether it avoids the identity sin^2+cos^2=1 and any step that already uses a^2+b^2=c^2.

## Observations for the "unexplored mechanisms" task

1. The 2023-2025 literature clusters around one idea (JJ): use an identity (double-angle or addition formula) that can be proved geometrically without Pythagoras, then apply it to a construction where two sides of an equation appear. Variants: infinite series (JJ, Luzia 2), no series (Kise et al.), and tangent-sum (Lengvárszky 2018).
2. Self-similarity (geometric series of similar copies) appears in Lengvárszky 2013, JJ 2023, Bhatnagar-Shrivastava 2023/24, Shene 2023/25, and Luzia's iteration. It is a mature family, with a general principle: Area(A) = Area(A-B)/(1-rho^2).
3. Parametrised area families: Alsina-Nelsen tiling (uncountable), Navas ziggurats (angle theta), Navas equilateral triangles. All reduce to Euclid VI.31 plus an area constant C(theta).
4. Analytic-style mechanisms that are rarer: Cavallina's shape-derivative/Hadamard (variational), Shene's L'Hopital/power-series/Euler (analysis, but for the identity), the integral of sin x cos x (Molokach), and Chakraborty's use of Heron.
5. Possibly under-explored (no proof found in this pass): inversion in a circle, Mobius/complex-analytic proofs, an optimisation/variational (min or max) proof, a probabilistic or measure-based proof, an information/entropy argument, a proof through a group or Lie rotation (R(theta) preserves length is the content of the theorem), and a pure algebraic proof of the Euclidean norm from the parallelogram law. These are open gaps, not claims of novelty; each needs a literature check.
