7. **Granularity of the Lean axioms.** The brief allows similarity, area additivity, Thales, intersecting chords and congruence as non-circular principles. I treat those classes (HL congruence, AA similarity of right triangles, the rectangle) as axioms. Every lemma from the C1 audit (equal tangents, the square at the vertex, the half-angle, the homothety ratio, the algebra) is then proved from them as a separate theorem. A derivation from Hilbert's or Tarski's axioms would need the whole theory of proportion, and that is out of scope.
8. **Angle measure.** I use Birkhoff-style degrees (`ang : P → P → P → ℝ`), with the junk value 0 for degenerate angles. This lets me state angle addition and linear pairs without a separate theory of angle congruence.
9. **Existence axioms are stated for right triangles only.** The incircle and A-excircle axioms assume `ang A C B = 90`. They are special cases of the general theorem that every triangle has an incircle and excircles. I chose this so that I don't need a non-collinearity primitive.
10. **Consistency is checked numerically, not formally.** A formal Lean model would have to use Mathlib's Euclidean geometry, which the brief bans from the formalization. Instead, `lean/model_check.py` tests every axiom on 20,000 random Euclidean instances each.
11. **Lean warnings.** Mathlib now suggests the `module` system. The resulting "consider adding `module`" warnings are harmless and I left them.
12. **External review (user-supplied).** I accepted the reviewer's three novelty objections and downgraded C1 from NEW to LIKELY KNOWN (REPORT §5a).
